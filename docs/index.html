The school gets paid.
Nobody else learns a thing.

A parent abroad sends tuition. The school settles it only if the money was sent for something it accepts. Midnight checks that rule inside a zero-knowledge proof, so the chain never sees who sent it, how much, or what for.

Who sees what.

On a public chain, enforcing "tuition only" means publishing every family's payment. Here the rule is enforced on data that stays on the family's device.

Three calls, one rule.

Everything private comes in as a witness and never leaves the device. Only the values marked with disclose() reach the ledger.

  1. The parent seals

    School, purpose, amount and a random nonce are hashed on the device. The proof shows the amount is above zero and under the cap.

    seal() → commitment
  2. The school settles

    It proves it is the school named inside one of the sealed remittances, that it accepts the hidden purpose, and that this one was never settled.

    settle() → school id, root, nullifier
  3. An auditor checks one

    The family hands over a single receipt. The auditor recomputes the same hashes and finds it sealed and settled. The rest stay hashes.

    commitmentOf(), nullifierOf()

What this does not claim.

  • Money does not move yetSettling authorises a payout. Wiring it to Midnight's shielded tokens is the next step.
  • The school is visible when it settlesInstitutions are public by design, and settlement timing could hint at enrolment periods.
  • Purpose is declared by the senderThe proof shows the declared purpose is one the school accepts, not how the money was spent.
  • Auditing is voluntaryThe family chooses to hand over a receipt. A mandatory regulator view key is not built.

Where this comes from. Kirogi first settled remittances on Creditcoin for BUIDL CTC 2026 Fall, with every payment public. This is a new implementation on Midnight, written for the Midnight Korea Hackathon 2026: the same rule, enforced on data the chain never sees.
